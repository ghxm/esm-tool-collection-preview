Tools for Mobile Surveying and Sensing

More and more social science researchers are discovering the potential of smartphones as a device for data collection ranging from mobile surveying to mobile sensing. Accordingly, a diverse landscape of tools (software services, technical frameworks) targeting these needs has emerged, but is at the same time characterized by heterogeneous terms and diverse actors, making it difficult to navigate.

With this tool review, we aim to generate a comprehensive yet focused list of tools that enable the collection of intensive-longitudinal data using mobile technologies to study social science phenomena. If you are interested in contributing to the tool review, check out the How to contribute for more information.

What do we mean by intensive-longitudinal?

We broadly define intensive-longitudinal methods as a set of research techniques used to collect detailed and frequent data on individuals. These research techniques are characterized by a) high frequency of data collection which means that data is gathered at very short intervals, be it daily, hourly, or even more frequently. This allows for the capture of dynamic processes and changes as they occur in real-time. Intensive-longitudinal designs often involve b) collecting rich, multi-faceted data including self-reports, behavioral observations, or environmental assessments. The primary goal of these methods is c) to understand intra-individual variability and the processes that drive change within individuals over time. This contrasts with traditional longitudinal methods that often focus more on inter-individual differences and long-term trends.

What data types are covered?

Intensive-longitudinal data can be generated both through active self-reporting and passive behavioral tracing, often utilizing sensors built into mobile devices to capture contextual data. Self-reported data refer to any responses of study participants provided via questionnaires, experience sampling or diary methods. Sensor data refer to data passively collected by technical systems without participants having to actively provide content. A promising avenue for future social science research lies not only in the combination of different types of data, but also in their ongoing integration or synchronization, where smartphone-mediated or real-world behavior serves as a trigger for self-reporting.

Which technologies are included?

Implementing these research designs requires suitable tools or technical framework for data collection. With this tool review, we aim at providing a comprehensive, easy-to-approach data base on existing mobile sensing tools, their scope and target groups. We limit our review on tools that make use of the smartphone for data collection, more precisely for collecting sensor logs, device logs, or usage logs (Schödel & Mehl, 2024) as well as pure but repeated self-reports. The review also covers tools to collect external sensor logs, e.g. from smart watches, but only if these log data are then transferred to the researcher via smartphone.

Why are we doing this?

With this tool list, we have a least three applications in mind:

  1. For researchers: The tool list offers researchers, especially those with less experience in this field, an easy way to get started in the field of mobile data collections.
  2. For developers: The tool list provides potential individual or organizational developers of such tools with a general market overview, making it easier to decide whether development resources are best invested in custom in-house or in collaborative development projects.
  3. For funders: The tool list results in a solid and growing data set that enables research funders and infrastructure providers to make informed decisions when aiming at building a sustainable tool landscape together.
